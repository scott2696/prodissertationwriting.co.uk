Build variance: the same slot sold at two different prices

This is the most consequential thing on this page and most players have never heard of it. Studios such as Pragmatic Play, NetEnt, Play'n GO and Red Tiger ship many of their slots in multiple RTP configurations. The operator chooses which to license. The game name, artwork, mechanics and maximum win are identical; only the return changes.

How to check a game's real RTP in ten seconds

  1. Open the game in real-money mode. Demo mode sometimes runs a different configuration, so check the version you will actually play.
  2. Open the information panel. It is the i icon, or the paytable button, or the burger menu depending on the studio. Every certified game has one and it is required to carry the figure.
  3. Scroll to the last page of the paytable. The RTP is almost always stated at the bottom, in small text, under a heading like “Return to player” or “Theoretical RTP”.
  4. Compare it to the studio's published figure. A quick search for the game name and “RTP” gives you the best available build. If the panel shows a lower number, this casino has licensed the cut version.
  5. If the panel shows a range, read it carefully. “94.0% – 96.5%” usually means the operator can switch configurations, and does not tell you which is live. Treat it as the bottom of the range.

Check the game you personally play. That is a better use of your time than trusting any list, including mine.

Why jackpot slots are the lowest payout games in the building

A progressive jackpot is funded by a levy on every spin. That money has to come from somewhere, and where it comes from is the base game's return. A slot advertising a £4 million progressive might run a base RTP of 88% to 92%, against 96% for its non-progressive equivalent — which makes it two to three times more expensive per hour.

The counter-argument is that the jackpot is part of the RTP, and technically that is true: the published figure usually includes the jackpot contribution. But it is only true in aggregate across everyone playing. For any individual player, the expected value tied up in a jackpot they will almost certainly never win behaves like money they will not get back. If you enjoy the possibility, buy it knowingly — just do not confuse it with a high payout game.

Payout percentage is not the same as winning

Two slots can both return 96% and feel completely different. Volatility — how the return is distributed — is what you actually experience:

  • Low volatility: frequent small wins, a balance that drains slowly and predictably. Your money lasts longest here. Best if you want playing time.
  • High volatility: long dry spells punctuated by rare large wins. Same long-run return, far wider outcomes. Best if you want a small chance of a big result and accept losing the stake quickly most of the time.

Neither is better value; they cost the same per pound staked. But volatility interacts badly with bonus wagering: a high-volatility game can wipe out a bonus balance long before the turnover requirement is met, which is why the sensible way to clear a wagering requirement is a low-volatility slot at the smallest qualifying stake. High payout casinos: your questions

Which online casino has the highest payout percentage?

No casino has a payout percentage — individual games do, and the operator chooses which build of each game to serve. Of the sites I test, Kingdom Casino gives access to the widest range of studios serving standard builds, which is the closest honest answer. The larger point is that your game choice matters far more than your casino choice: live blackjack at 99.5% against a 94% turbo slot is a difference no operator can offset.

What is a good RTP for an online slot?

96% or above is good, 95% to 96% is ordinary, and below 95% is expensive. Above 97% is rare and worth seeking out. Always check the figure in the game's own information panel rather than trusting a listing, because many slots ship in several RTP configurations and the operator decides which one you get. A 2.5 percentage point gap between builds of the same game raises your cost per £1,000 staked from about £35 to about £60.

Do casinos change the RTP of their games?

They cannot change a certified game's RTP, but they can choose which certified configuration to license, and many studios ship two or three. That is the build variance problem. The game name and artwork stay identical while the return changes. This is legal, disclosed in the information panel, and almost never mentioned anywhere a player would see it. Check the panel of the game you play most often.

Are high RTP slots better than jackpot slots?

For cost per hour, decisively yes. Progressive jackpot slots typically run a base RTP of 88% to 94% because the jackpot levy is funded out of the base game, making them two to three times more expensive than a 96% non-progressive. What you buy with that premium is a very small chance of a life-changing sum. That is a legitimate thing to want, but it is not a high payout game.

Can I see the RTP before I play?

Yes, in every certified game. Open the information or paytable panel and scroll to the final page: the theoretical RTP is stated there, usually in small print. If the panel shows a range rather than a single figure, the operator can switch configurations and you should assume the bottom of the range. Check it in real-money mode, because demo builds occasionally differ.

Does a higher RTP mean I will win?

No. RTP is a long-run average across millions of rounds, and any individual session can land anywhere. What a higher RTP reliably changes is how much your entertainment costs: at 96.5% a £1,000 turnover costs about £35, at 94% it costs about £60. You are buying a lower price, not a better chance of finishing ahead. Volatility, not RTP, determines how a session feels.
