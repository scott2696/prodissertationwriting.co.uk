What each welcome bonus actually costs to clear

This is the section that makes this page different from every other “best online casinos” list, so it is worth explaining the arithmetic before the table.

A welcome bonus has a wagering requirement: a multiple of the bonus, or of the deposit plus the bonus, that you must stake before the balance becomes withdrawable. Staking is not losing — but staking has an expected cost, because every pound you stake goes through a game with a house edge. At a typical 96% slot RTP, staking £1,000 costs about £40 in expectation. So the real question about any offer is simple: is the bonus bigger than the expected cost of the turnover needed to release it?

Is it legal for me to play at these casinos?

The legal position, checked against the Act

Yes. The Gambling Act 2005 regulates operators, not players. There is no offence in British law committed by an adult in Great Britain who gambles at an overseas online casino, and there never has been. The obligations in the Act fall on anyone providing facilities for gambling to British consumers — which is why an operator without a UKGC licence advertising to you is the one with the legal problem, not you.

Two practical consequences follow, and they matter more than the legality question people usually ask. First, because the operator is outside the UK system, the Gambling Commission cannot act for you in a dispute — you are dealing with the Curaçao Gaming Control Board or the Anjouan Gaming Authority, whose processes are slower and whose sanctions are weaker. Second, your winnings are still tax-free: the UK does not tax gambling winnings for recreational players, whether the operator is licensed in Westminster or Willemstad. The two narrow exceptions are covered on the gambling tax page, and the wider legal framework on UK gambling laws.

Do I pay tax on my winnings?

No. Recreational gambling winnings are not taxable income in the UK, and that does not change because the operator is licensed offshore. Gambling duty is levied on operators, not players. There is no threshold to declare and nothing to put on a self-assessment return. The two narrow situations where things get more complicated — trading as a professional, and holding winnings in cryptocurrency, which can create a separate capital gains event when you convert — are explained on the gambling tax page.

Will my UK bank block the deposit?

It might, and this is the most common thing that goes wrong. Most UK banks offer a gambling block, some enable it by default on certain account types, and Monzo, Starling, Lloyds, Halifax, NatWest and others all support one. When it is on, the payment simply fails without a useful explanation. Check your banking app's gambling settings first. Some banks also enforce a cooling-off period of up to 48 hours before a block can be lifted, which is by design.
