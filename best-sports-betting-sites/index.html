How to read a bookmaker's margin

The overround is the amount by which a bookmaker's implied probabilities exceed 100%. It is the price of the bet and it is completely visible if you know where to look. Converting odds to implied probability takes one division: 1 divided by the decimal odds.

Take a football match priced 2.10 / 3.40 / 3.75:

Calculating the overround on a three-way football market
SelectionDecimal oddsImplied probability
Home2.1047.6%
Draw3.4029.4%
Away3.7526.7%
Total—103.7%

That 3.7% above 100 is the bookmaker's margin on the market. Do the same sum at another site and you can compare them directly. As a guide for UK punters:

Market depth and in-play

Market count is a vanity metric — “200+ markets per match” usually means a long tail of player props nobody bets. What matters for a typical UK punter is a much shorter list:

  • Asian handicaps and totals with quarter lines. These are the lowest-margin markets any bookmaker offers, frequently under 3%. If a site prices them, that is where the value is.
  • Bet builders and same-game multiples. Convenient and expensive — the margin on a four-leg same-game builder is routinely above 20%. Fun, but price them as entertainment.
  • In-play with a usable cash-out. Check the latency and the suspension behaviour before you rely on it. Offshore in-play is generally slower to update than the major UK books.
  • Early prices on racing. Rare offshore. If you take prices in the morning, this matters to you more than anything else on this page.

Free bets, and what they are worth

A £30 free bet is not worth £30. A free bet usually returns winnings only — the stake is not returned — so its real value is roughly its face value multiplied by the odds minus one, divided by the odds. At even money that is about half; at 4.0 it is about 75%.

Which yields the one piece of genuinely useful free bet advice: use a free bet on a longer-priced selection than you would normally back, because the stake is not yours and the stake-not-returned structure rewards higher odds. And weigh the whole offer against the margin point above — a one-off £20 of value at a site with a 7% overround is a poor trade against permanent access to a 4% book.

What is an overround and why does it matter?

It is the amount by which a bookmaker's implied probabilities exceed 100%, and it is the price you pay for the bet. Convert each price to implied probability by dividing 1 by the decimal odds, add them up, and subtract 100. A Premier League market under 104% is keen; over 106% is poor. It matters because it applies to every bet you place, for ever, whereas a free bet applies once.

Are free bets worth anything?

Roughly half to three-quarters of their face value, because a free bet normally returns winnings only and not the stake. A £30 free bet used at evens is worth about £15; used at odds of 5.00 it is worth about £24. That is why the sensible use of a free bet is on a longer price than you would normally back. Weigh it against the margin: permanent access to a keener book beats a one-off £20 of value within months.

Can I bet on horse racing at these sites?

Yes, but this is where offshore sportsbooks are weakest. UK and Irish racing is a specialist product, and these books typically offer thinner markets, later prices, few early prices and no Best Odds Guaranteed. If racing is your main sport, UK-licensed bookmakers serve you better on price, quite apart from the regulatory protections. Football is where these sites are genuinely competitive.

Do these betting sites offer cash out?

Most do, but check the latency and the suspension behaviour before relying on it. Offshore in-play prices generally update more slowly than the major UK books, and a cash-out figure is priced with its own margin on top — often a substantial one. Cash out is a convenience you pay for, not a way of getting a better price, and the fee is invisible because it is inside the number you are shown.

How long do betting withdrawals take?

The same as the casino side, because it is the same cashier: hours for crypto, two to five working days for a debit card. Your first withdrawal triggers identity verification and will be slower. Note that settlement is separate from withdrawal — most markets settle within minutes of an event ending, but voided, abandoned or disputed events can take considerably longer, and the rules for those vary between operators.

Is betting at an offshore site legal in the UK?

Yes for you. The Gambling Act 2005 places its obligations on operators rather than on players, so no offence is committed by an adult in Great Britain placing a bet at an overseas site. What you lose is protection rather than legality: no Gambling Commission complaints route, no GamStop, and none of the UK requirements on advertising, dispute resolution or dormant accounts.
