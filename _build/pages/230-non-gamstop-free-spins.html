The cap is the number that matters

A conversion cap sets the ceiling on the entire offer regardless of what the spins do. If it is £100, then 450 spins and 4,500 spins are worth exactly the same at the top end. When an operator advertises spin count prominently and the cap in a footnote, that ordering tells you which number they would rather you compared on. Find the cap before you find anything else.

The five terms that decide the value

  1. Value per spin. Usually 10p, sometimes 20p or 30p. Multiply by the count to get face value — this is the only way to compare packages meaningfully.
  2. Wagering on winnings. Most spin winnings become a bonus balance requiring 30x to 45x staking. A handful pay straight to cash; those are worth several times more.
  3. Maximum conversion. The hard ceiling. Usually £50 or £100, and applied regardless of spin count.
  4. The nominated game. Spins are almost always locked to one slot chosen by the operator, and its RTP and volatility are not your choice. Check the RTP in the game panel.
  5. Release schedule and expiry. Large packages typically drip out at 30 to 50 spins a day over one to two weeks, with each tranche expiring in 24 hours. Miss a day and those spins are gone — which is the mechanic that makes a large package a daily-return product rather than a gift.

No-wagering spins: the ones actually worth having

A small number of offers pay spin winnings straight to your withdrawable cash balance with no wagering at all. These are genuinely valuable and worth taking wherever you find them — a 20-spin no-wagering offer can be worth more than a 200-spin offer at 40x, because you keep what you win.

They are rarer offshore than at UK-licensed sites, and they usually come with a lower spin count and a conversion cap that is still worth checking. The distinguishing phrase in the terms is “winnings paid as cash” or “no wagering requirement”; anything that says winnings are “credited as bonus funds” is the ordinary kind.

Free spins: your questions

What are free spins actually worth?

Multiply the spin count by the value per spin to get face value — 450 spins at 10p is £45 of staking, returning about £43 in expectation at 96% RTP. Then apply the wagering requirement on the winnings and the conversion cap, and the realistic expected value falls to a few pounds. The cap sets the absolute ceiling: if it is £100, no spin package can pay you more than £100 however large the headline.

Are 450 free spins better than 150?

Not necessarily, and often not. What matters is face value, wagering and the cap. 450 spins at 10p and 150 spins at 30p are both £45 of staking; if the smaller package carries 30x wagering against the larger one's 40x, the smaller package is the better offer despite advertising a number one third the size. Compare on value per spin and the conversion cap, never on the count.

Do free spins have wagering requirements?

Almost always. Winnings from the spins are credited as bonus funds which must then be staked 30 to 45 times before becoming withdrawable, and a conversion cap limits the cash you can realise on top of that. The exception is "no wagering" spins, where winnings pay straight to your cash balance — genuinely valuable, considerably rarer offshore, and worth more than several times their spin count in the ordinary kind.

Why do I only get 30 free spins a day?

Because large packages are almost always released in daily tranches rather than all at once, typically 30 to 50 spins a day over one to two weeks, with each day's allocation expiring within 24 hours. That is the mechanic that turns a headline number into a daily-return product. If you miss a day, those spins are gone. Check the release schedule before claiming — a package requiring fifteen consecutive daily logins is a different proposition from one credited immediately.

Can I choose which game to use free spins on?

Rarely. Spins are usually locked to a single slot nominated by the operator, chosen by them rather than by you, which means its RTP and volatility are not your decision. Check the game's RTP in its information panel — some nominated titles run noticeably below the studio's best build. If the spins are locked to a high-volatility slot, expect most claims to return nothing, which is what the distribution of that kind of game does.

Can I withdraw free spins winnings?

Yes, once any wagering requirement on the winnings is met and subject to the conversion cap, and with a fully verified account. Attempting to withdraw before wagering is complete usually forfeits the bonus balance entirely. Check also whether the offer requires a qualifying deposit before winnings can be paid out, which many do even where the spins themselves were free.
