Best odds betting sites UK: how to read a bookmaker's margin

The overround is the amount by which a bookmaker's implied probabilities exceed 100%. It is the price of the bet and it is completely visible if you know where to look. Converting odds to implied probability takes one division: 1 divided by the decimal odds.

Take a football match priced 2.10 / 3.40 / 3.75:

Calculating the overround on a three-way football market
SelectionDecimal oddsImplied probability
Home2.1047.6%
Draw3.4029.4%
Away3.7526.7%
Total—103.7%

That 3.7% above 100 is the bookmaker's margin on the market. Do the same sum at another site and you can compare them directly. As a guide for UK punters:

The best betting site for football accumulators, and what an acca really costs

Accumulators are the most popular bet in Britain and the most expensive. The margin compounds across every leg: five selections at 4% each do not give you 4%, they give you roughly 22%, because each leg is priced with its own cut. A £10 five-fold costs about £2.20 in expectation against roughly 50p for the same opinions backed as singles.

So the honest answer to which site is best for accas is the one with the keenest prices on the legs you actually want, because that is the only lever you control — acca insurance and bonus ladders are marketing built on top of a margin that has already been taken. Market depth, in-play and the best cash out betting sites

Market count is a vanity metric — “200+ markets per match” usually means a long tail of player props nobody bets. What matters for a typical UK punter is a much shorter list:

  • Asian handicaps and totals with quarter lines. These are the lowest-margin markets any bookmaker offers, frequently under 3%. If a site prices them, that is where the value is.
  • Bet builders and same-game multiples. Convenient and expensive — the margin on a four-leg same-game builder is routinely above 20%. Fun, but price them as entertainment.

  • Early prices on racing. Rare offshore. If you take prices in the morning, this matters to you more than anything else on this page.

Betting sites with free bets, and what a free bet is really worth

A £30 free bet is not worth £30. A free bet usually returns winnings only — the stake is not returned — so its real value is roughly its face value multiplied by the odds minus one, divided by the odds. At even money that is about half; at 4.0 it is about 75%.

What is an overround and why does it matter?

It is the amount by which a bookmaker's implied probabilities exceed 100%, and it is the price you pay for the bet. Convert each price to implied probability by dividing 1 by the decimal odds, add them up, and subtract 100. A Premier League market under 104% is keen; over 106% is poor. It matters because it applies to every bet you place, for ever, whereas a free bet applies once.

What is acca insurance, and is it worth anything?

Acca insurance refunds your stake, usually as a free bet, when one leg of a multiple lets you down. It is genuinely useful and it is also priced in: the margin on an accumulator compounds across every leg, so a five-fold at 4% per leg carries roughly 22% total, and the insurance is funded out of that. Read the conditions closely — minimum legs, minimum odds per leg, and whether the refund is cash or a free bet, because a stake-not-returned free bet is worth roughly half its face value at even money.

What is in-play betting, and how does it differ from pre-match?

In-play betting is placing bets while the event is running, with prices updating continuously. Two things change against pre-match. Margins are wider, typically by two to three percentage points, because you are paying for immediacy. And the feed matters: offshore books update a little more slowly than the major UK operators, which means more market suspensions and the occasional rejected bet when the price moves between your click and the confirmation. It is genuinely useful when you are watching and hold a view the market has not caught up with.
