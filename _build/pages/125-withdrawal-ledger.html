What the ledger shows

Three findings hold across all 74 requests. Crypto beats cards at every single operator, without exception — the gap between methods is larger than the gap between the best and worst casino, which means your payment choice matters more than your casino choice. The first withdrawal is always the slow one, because it triggers identity verification; the honest measure of an operator's speed is its second request onwards. And the worst case matters more than the median: a site with a four-hour median and a nine-day outlier is a different proposition from one that is consistently slow, and the table below shows both.

The ledger

How these timings are measured

  • The clock starts when I press withdraw and stops when cleared funds are in my account or wallet. Not when the operator marks the request “processed”, which is a different and consistently flattering number.
  • Every request is made from my own verified account, funded with my own money, from a UK connection.
  • Failed and cancelled requests are logged too. A request that had to be resubmitted counts from the first attempt, because that is what it cost me in real time.
  • First withdrawals are flagged separately, because they include verification time and would otherwise distort every median upwards.
  • Nothing here is supplied by an operator. No casino has been given advance notice of a test, and no figure on this page comes from a help page or an affiliate manager.
What this data is not

It is one person's account history, not a statistically representative sample of all customers. My accounts are modest, my withdrawals are ordinary sums, and I have never triggered the kind of enhanced review that a very large win attracts — which is precisely the scenario where operators differ most and where I have no data. Treat these figures as a floor: a casino that is slow with me will not be fast with you, but a casino that is fast with me has not thereby proved how it behaves when someone wins £50,000.

How to read it

Compare within a method, never across methods. A card median of three days is not worse than a crypto median of four hours; they are different rails. The question the ledger answers is whether this operator is quick on the method you intend to use.

Look at the worst case before the median. Medians hide tails. An operator with a two-hour median and a six-day worst case has a process that sometimes fails; one with a one-day median and a two-day worst case is consistent. Which you prefer depends on whether you value speed or predictability, but you cannot make that choice from a median alone.

Discount the first request. It contains verification. Every operator looks worse on it, and no operator's real performance is measured by it.

The entries I would rather not publish

Publishing only the good results would make this page useless, so the failures are in the table alongside everything else. When a request needed chasing, the ledger says so. When an operator's worst case is materially worse than its median, that gap is visible rather than smoothed away. When I have too few requests at an operator to draw a conclusion, the row says that too, rather than presenting one data point as a median.

If you have had a materially different experience at any of these casinos — faster or slower — I would genuinely like to know. Send it through the contact page with the method and the dates. I cannot add other people's timings to a ledger of my own requests, but repeated reports of a problem are a reason for me to go and test it again, and I will say so on the page when that happens.
