James McKean

Founder and lead reviewer

I spent six years in card acquiring and chargeback operations in Manchester, most of it on gambling merchant accounts. That job is the reason this site exists. From the acquiring side you see exactly why one operator's withdrawals clear in four hours and another's sit for a week, and none of it is visible from the customer side — the customer just sees a status that says “pending” and a live chat agent reading a script.

So I started measuring from the outside. I open every account on this site in my own name, at my own address, from a home connection. I fund it with my own money. I read the full terms document rather than the summary box on the promotion page. Then I request withdrawals at different amounts and at different times of day, and I write down when cleared funds actually arrive — not when the operator marks the request processed, which is a different and much friendlier number. That record is published in full, including the bad entries, in the withdrawal ledger.

The other half of my job is the arithmetic. Chargeback work teaches you to read a contract for the clause that will be used against someone later, and casino bonus terms are written the same way. Every welcome offer on this site is modelled from the operator's own wagering terms to work out what it costs to clear, which is why this site keeps telling people to decline bonuses it is paid to promote.

Writes about: online casinos, withdrawal testing, UK payment methods, bonus terms and wagering arithmetic, crypto casinos, sports betting and odds, casino reviews.

Lisa Brown

Editor, regulation and compliance

Lisa spent eight years in financial services compliance, latterly writing the consumer-facing disclosures that had to survive a regulator reading them line by line. She writes the legal, regulatory and tax pages here, and fact-checks every other page before it is published.

That means three specific jobs. The first is reading the full terms and conditions behind every offer quoted anywhere on this site — which is how the maximum-conversion and maximum-bet clauses that appear throughout the bonus pages got there, since neither is ever in the advertisement. The second is verifying every licence claim against the issuing regulator's own register rather than the badge in a site's footer, and checking that the licensed company is the same company named in the operator's terms. The third is signing off every statement this site makes about the law, tax or responsible gambling.

Lisa is also the reason each non-GamStop page opens by asking self-excluded readers to leave rather than burying that further down. It costs this business traffic and conversions, and it is not up for discussion.

Writes about: UK gambling law, the Gambling Act 2005, UKGC licence conditions, GamStop, offshore licensing regimes, gambling taxation, bonus terms, responsible gambling.

Before this: eight years in financial services compliance and consumer disclosure.

Note: nothing on this site is legal or tax advice. It is general information, written carefully, and it is not a substitute for advice on your own circumstances.

Why bylines matter on a site like this

Affiliate gambling pages are overwhelmingly anonymous, and that is not an accident. An unnamed author cannot be asked where a number came from, cannot be held to a previous claim, and cannot be embarrassed by a correction. Putting two real names on every page removes all three of those conveniences, which is exactly the point.

It also means the division of labour is visible. James writes the operator-facing pages because he is the one with money in the cashiers; Lisa writes the legal, tax and regulatory pages because she is the one who reads statutes for a living. Neither of us writes the other's section, and each of us checks the other's work before it is published.

What we hold ourselves to

  1. No claim without a source. A statute, a regulator's register, an operator's published terms, or a measurement one of us made.
  2. No score we cannot derive. Every rating is computed from the five weights published on the methodology page.
  3. No invented data. If a withdrawal was not made, the ledger says so rather than estimating.
  4. Corrections are public and dated. We do not silently amend a page.
  5. Commercial relationships are disclosed on every page, and paid placement is labelled as advertising.
  6. Nothing is written by an outside contributor. If that ever changes, they will be named here first.
