Real crypto balances versus fiat shadow balances

There are two kinds of site calling themselves crypto casinos and the difference matters.

A true crypto casino holds your balance in the coin. You deposit 0.5 LTC, your balance reads 0.5 LTC, you bet in LTC. If the coin doubles overnight your balance doubles; if it halves, so does your money.

A fiat shadow casino converts on arrival. You send crypto, it is converted at the operator's rate into a pound or euro balance, and you play in that currency. Your balance is then stable, but you have paid a conversion spread on the way in and you will pay it again on the way out.

Neither is wrong, but you should know which you are using. The shadow model is better for most UK players, because it removes market risk from a session and lets you reason about stakes in pounds. The conversion spread is the cost of that, typically 1% to 3% each way and rarely disclosed as a fee. If you want the stability without the spread, deposit a stablecoin into a true crypto casino — you get a balance that does not move and no conversion at either end.

Provably fair, explained properly

“Provably fair” is a real cryptographic technique and a heavily abused marketing phrase, so it is worth being precise about what it does and does not cover.

The mechanism: before a round, the casino commits to a secret server seed by publishing its hash. You supply a client seed. The outcome is derived from both. Afterwards the server seed is revealed, and you can confirm the hash matches and re-derive the result yourself. Because the casino committed before knowing your seed, it could not have altered the outcome after the fact.

What it genuinely proves is that a specific round was not tampered with after you bet. What it does not prove, and what people routinely assume it does:

  • It does not mean a fair price. A provably fair dice game can carry a 10% house edge and be entirely provably fair. The technique verifies the process, not the odds.
  • It does not cover third-party games. Slots from Pragmatic Play, NetEnt and the rest are not provably fair and cannot be — they run on the studio's servers and rely on independent certification instead. A casino advertising provably fair games usually means its handful of in-house originals.
  • It does not mean the casino will pay you. Verifiable outcomes and a functioning cashier are unrelated problems. Every payout dispute I have seen was about the cashier, not the game.

The capital gains trap

Checked against HMRC's position on cryptoassets

Your gambling winnings are not taxable in the UK. That is settled and it does not change because the operator is offshore. But cryptocurrency is treated as an asset, not as money. If you withdraw winnings in Bitcoin and later convert them to pounds, you have disposed of an asset, and any increase in its value between the moment you acquired it and the moment you sold it is a capital gain. The gambling win is untaxed; the subsequent crypto movement may not be.

A worked example makes it concrete. You withdraw 0.1 BTC when Bitcoin is £50,000, so you have received £5,000 of tax-free winnings. Three months later Bitcoin is £65,000 and you sell. You receive £6,500. The £5,000 remains untaxed as a gambling win; the £1,500 increase is a capital gain and counts against your annual exempt amount. Whether any tax is actually due depends on that allowance and on your other disposals in the year.

Two consequences follow. First, keep a record of the date, the coin and the sterling value at the moment each withdrawal arrives — that is your acquisition cost, and reconstructing it a year later is unpleasant. Second, if you want to avoid the issue entirely, use a stablecoin and convert promptly: a coin pegged to the dollar that you sell within days has essentially no gain to report beyond currency movement. Getting started from a UK bank account

  1. Open an account at an FCA-registered exchange. Kraken, Coinbase and Bitstamp are all registered with the Financial Conduct Authority for UK customers. Registration is about money-laundering supervision rather than consumer protection, but an unregistered exchange is a worse starting point.
  2. Verify there first. Exchange identity checks are more thorough than casino ones, and doing them before you need them avoids a delay at the worst time.
  3. Fund it by bank transfer, not card. Faster Payments into an exchange is usually free; card funding carries a fee of several per cent. Note that some UK banks restrict or block transfers to exchanges, and that is a bank policy you cannot argue with.

  6. Record the sterling value on arrival. Date, coin, amount, value in pounds. This is your acquisition cost for capital gains purposes and takes ten seconds now.

What actually goes wrong

  • Wrong network. The big one. Funds sent on a network the casino does not support are usually unrecoverable.
  • A balance that moved. Deposit in Bitcoin, play for a week, and your untouched balance can be worth 15% less. Stablecoins remove this entirely.
  • Withdrawal minimums in coin terms. A minimum expressed in BTC can be a meaningful sum in pounds, stranding a small balance. Check it before you deposit.
  • A bank that blocks the exchange transfer. Several UK banks restrict payments to crypto exchanges. This happens before the casino is involved at all.
  • Conversion spread you did not see. On shadow-balance sites, 1–3% each way, disclosed as a rate rather than a fee.
