House edge and RTP: what a session at online casino sites UK really costs

RTP — return to player — is the percentage of everything staked on a game that the game returns to players over an enormous number of rounds. A 96% slot has a 4% house edge. That edge is not a fee taken once; it is applied to every pound you stake, including the pounds you are re-staking out of winnings.

This is why turnover, not deposit, is the number that determines what a session costs. Consider a £50 deposit played at £0.50 a spin on a 96% slot:

What a £50 deposit costs, by how long you play it
Spins playedTotal stakedExpected cost at 96% RTPExpected balance left
100 spins£50£2£48

1,000 spins£500£20£30
2,500 spins£1,250£50£0

Two things fall out of that table. The first is that a £50 deposit does not cost £50 — it costs whatever you stake times the edge, and it is gone when cumulative turnover reaches roughly twenty-five times the deposit. The second is that variance is what you actually experience: the table shows the average of many sessions, and no individual session looks like it. Some end at zero in ten minutes and a few end well up. The expected cost is the price of the entertainment; the variance is the entertainment.

The one lever you control

You cannot change the RTP of a game, but you can change how fast you buy turnover. Stake size and spin speed are the two levers. Halving your stake halves your hourly cost exactly. Turning off turbo spin roughly halves it again. A player who moves from £1 turbo spins to 50p standard spins on the same game has cut their cost per hour by about 75% without changing anything about what they are playing.

How a welcome bonus works, honestly

A welcome bonus is a loan of house money with a turnover condition attached. You cannot withdraw until you have staked a multiple of it, and that staking has an expected cost. So the relevant question is never “how big is the bonus” but “does the bonus exceed the expected cost of the turnover required to free it”.

Four terms determine the answer, and all four are in the small print rather than the headline:

  • The multiple. 35x is workable. 45x rarely is. 60x is a decoration.
  • The base. “35x bonus” and “35x deposit + bonus” differ by roughly a factor of two in required turnover for a 100% match.
  • The conversion cap. The maximum that can become withdrawable cash. A large headline with a small cap is a small offer.
  • Game weighting. Slots usually count 100%; live games often 10%, 5% or zero. Clearing a bonus at a live table is generally impossible by design.

I model all of this for every offer on this site. The priced version of each one is on the casino bonuses page, and the summary is blunt: most welcome offers cost more than they give. That is not fraud, it is arithmetic, and it is a perfectly reasonable thing to buy as long as you know you are buying it.

The cashier is the product: judging real money casino sites UK players use

Since the games are identical everywhere, the cashier is where casinos genuinely differ, and it is the part you only find out about at the worst possible moment — when you try to take money out. Four things to check before you deposit anywhere:

  • Does it hold GBP? A site that converts to euros charges roughly 2–4% on the way in and again on the way out. That spread is invisible and it is usually larger than any bonus you will clear.
  • What is the minimum withdrawal, and the maximum? Monthly withdrawal caps are common offshore and they are how a large win becomes an instalment plan. A £10,000 win against a £2,000 monthly cap takes five months.
  • When does verification happen? Always at the first withdrawal. Do it on day one instead.
  • Is there a fee? Usually not, but some sites charge for a second withdrawal within a period, or for card payouts.

Are online casinos rigged, and do gambling commission licensed casinos differ?

The games do not need to be rigged, which is the real answer. A 96% RTP slot is publicly, contractually designed to return 96% of turnover — the casino's profit is guaranteed by the maths, not by interference. Games from the major studios are certified by independent test houses and the operator cannot alter a certified game's RTP. The genuine risks offshore are different ones: a cashier that delays, a bonus term used to void a win, or a monthly withdrawal cap that turns a payout into instalments. Those are what I test for.

What does RTP mean?

Return to player: the percentage of total stakes a game returns to players across a very large number of rounds. A 96% RTP slot has a 4% house edge, so every £1,000 staked costs about £40 in expectation. It is a long-run average and says nothing about any individual session. Note that RTP applies to turnover, not to your deposit, which is why a £50 deposit re-staked repeatedly can cost far more than £50 would suggest.

Are online casinos legal in UK law?

Yes. The Gambling Act 2005 places its obligations on operators, not players, and no offence is committed by an adult in Great Britain gambling at an overseas site. What changes is your protection rather than your legality: the Gambling Commission cannot intervene in a dispute with a casino it does not license, and GamStop does not cover it. How do I check that an online casino's licence is real?

Do not trust the badge in the footer — click it, and then go further. A legitimate operator displays a licence number that resolves to the issuing regulator's own public register, showing a company name and a live status. Check that the company named on the register is the same company named in the site's terms and conditions; a mismatch between the two is the most common tell. If the badge links nowhere, links to a static image, or the number does not appear on the register, walk away before depositing rather than investigating after a dispute.

Quick verification vs “no docs” casinos: what is KYC and when does it happen?

KYC is the identity check anti-money-laundering law requires before an operator releases funds. A UKGC-licensed casino must verify you before you can deposit; offshore sites typically let you play immediately and verify at your first withdrawal instead, which feels lighter at signup and considerably heavier at cashout. A site advertising “no KYC” almost always means no checks until a withdrawal threshold, at which point full checks apply. A site that genuinely never verifies anyone is not running AML controls at all, and that is not a site you want holding your balance.

Who powers your favourite games, and does the software provider matter?

Casinos almost never build their own games; they license them from studios. Pragmatic Play, Evolution, NetEnt, Play'n GO, Nolimit City, Hacksaw Gaming, Relax Gaming and BGaming supply most of what you will see, and the same titles appear at site after site because they are the same games. That is why the studio list tells you more about a lobby than the game count does — and why the things that genuinely differ between casinos are the cashier, the bonus terms and the support desk rather than the catalogue.

How do online casinos work?

An online casino licenses games from studios such as Pragmatic Play and Evolution, hosts them behind a cashier, and takes a fixed mathematical margin on everything staked. The games are certified by independent test houses and the operator cannot alter a certified game's return, so the house does not need to interfere — the margin is built into the rules. You deposit, the balance sits with the operator, you stake it repeatedly, and the edge is applied to every pound of that turnover rather than once to your deposit. That is why a £50 deposit re-staked twenty-five times costs about the same as losing it outright.

What should I look for when trying out a new casino?

Six things, in this order: a licence number that resolves on the issuing regulator's own register and names the same company as the terms; a cashier that holds pounds rather than converting to euros; withdrawal terms, especially the weekly or monthly cap; the wagering multiple and what base it applies to; whether live chat answers a specific question competently before you deposit; and a small test round trip — deposit £20, play a little, withdraw £15. How do I find the best payment method for me?

Pick the method you want to be paid by, then deposit with it, because funds generally return along the route they arrived. If speed matters, that means cryptocurrency: hours rather than the two to five working days a debit card takes. If you want nothing to do with crypto, use a debit card or open banking, and check your bank's gambling block before you assume a decline is the casino's fault. Avoid methods that only work one way — Apple Pay and paysafecard deposit but cannot pay you out, so you will need a second method anyway. The full comparison is on the payment methods page.

What is the difference between a casino that is safe and one that is licensed?

A licence tells you who you can complain to; it does not tell you that the operator behaves well. A UK Gambling Commission licence is the strongest version available to a British player, because it carries GamStop, an approved dispute-resolution body, mandatory fund segregation and a regulator with the power to remove the licence. An offshore licence is a much thinner promise — the Malta regime is genuinely supervised, the reformed Curaçao regime is improving, and Anjouan is close to a registration. None of the sites here is UK-licensed, so “safe” on this page means tested and paying, not protected.
